# First working factory catalogue

Milestone 6 implementation values, authored 8 November 2026; inspection controls revised 12 November. Capabilities follow the [agreed plan](../plans/milestone-06-first-working-machines.md#agreed-scope-and-decisions--8-november-2026); every number below is provisional playtest balance. Reviewed runtime definitions are [factory.ts](../../src/content/factory.ts) and the existing [construction blueprints](../../src/content/first-factory.ts). Fibery owns connected authoring; changes require reconciliation into runtime and documentation.


The Factory tab is a read-only summary of placed equipment counts, power, restoration, weather, storage and water. E/primary click/controller west on a placed machine within two metres opens a separate dialog for enable/disable, recipes, buffers, wiring, material transport, utilities and recovery. Production and research continue while the dialog blocks world input. Solid input/output buffers use the player's inventory-slot presentation and retain empty slots to keep controls anchored. Closing, dismantling, losing reach or changing worlds releases inspection ownership.

## Equipable and local operation

All seven placed machine kinds use one 2 m grid cell, quarter-turn orientation and a 1.2 × 1.2 × 1.5 m logical box. Reach is **2 m to the nearest point of the cell footprint**, including corner approaches, for construction/configuration/transfers/dismantling. Player overlap checks the actual 0.35 m capsule radius against the 1.2 m machine collision body; the unused cell margin is not solid. The placement debug ring uses that same 2 m reach. The fixture retains its authored 10 × 10 cells; no free machines are placed at spawn. The extractor must anchor on one finite unclaimed scrap deposit. Other machines cannot overlap a live deposit. Models are original procedural prototype silhouettes in [machine-geometry.ts](../../src/game/views/machine-geometry.ts), with visible asymmetric material ports and a power terminal; they are presentation, not simulation geometry or finished external artwork.

**Equipable** has two kinds: **Tools**, such as the prybar, equip directly to the player and never enter the toolbar; **Placeables** are the seven factory machine kinds and power connections. Assign a carried placeable under Inventory & Crafting → Equipable to one of eight saved slots. Select with 1–8, controller left/right or the toolbar buttons, then B/Select toggles world building, R/right shoulder rotates machines and click/south button places. Machine previews use their silhouette and one-cell footprint. The worker resolves the sequenced active slot before spending one bag item. Slots reference stock rather than holding a second inventory; zero stock preserves the assignment. Opening a UI cancels build mode.

**Power connections use two clicks in build mode:** click a machine within 2 m to choose the first anchor, then click another machine to complete the wire. A live valid/invalid preview runs from the first machine's rotated power terminal to the targeted cell, snapping to the second terminal over a machine. Click the first cell again to cancel that attempt and choose a fresh anchor; this spends nothing and leaves build mode available. No connector is reserved or consumed by the first click. Completing requires a live distinct unconnected pair within 12 m, reach to either endpoint, researched electrical engineering, an active connector toolbar slot and one carried connector; the worker rechecks everything before consuming it. Switching slots/worlds, losing gameplay input, leaving build mode or removing the anchored machine clears the temporary draft. Draft anchors are presentation state and never enter saves. Factory retains global power statistics. Interacting with a nearby machine opens its live inspection dialog for disconnection and other local controls; new wiring uses the toolbar.

Toolbar slots and active index are optional additive player state in content `factory-1`. Valid known pre-toolbar saves default to an empty toolbar on detached data, preserving factories and native physics. Saved toolbar data is bounded/validated; tools and unknown items cannot occupy it. Existing control profiles gain missing toolbar bindings while preserving player rebindings and explicit unbinding.

Every assembly is safe to handcraft or request from the existing MiniFab. Its embodied scrap value is exactly the sum of its ingredients; no construction cost is folded into research payment. Basic supports exactly one distinct ingredient and Medium exactly two, using existing recipe work times. The recipe selector shows researched recipes compatible with that tier: Basic offers prybars and mechanical parts; Medium offers electrical parts, Basic research kits, power connections and Basic Fabricator assemblies. Quantities do not change the number of distinct ingredient types: four plates plus two mechanical parts is a two-input recipe. Four buffer slots store stacks; they do not grant four-input recipe support. This gives the tiers distinct roles and keeps the recycler → fabricator chain useful: personal deterministic plate/wire recovery recipes are excluded from factory fabrication. Higher-arity safe equipment assembly remains possible by hand/MiniFab. Ports are interfaces for Milestone 7, with no automatic material movement here.

Manual loading lists only materials accepted by the selected machine's configured process, once per item with its total across bag stacks. Fabricators use the applied recipe; a draft dropdown choice changes the controls only after Set recipe succeeds. With no applied recipe, a fabricator has no loading choices. The shared [material policy](../../src/content/machine-materials.ts) drives both inspection and worker validation. Accepted materials remain listed with zero available after depletion, keeping selection and layout stable. Labs may be prestocked with known research-cost materials; extractors and generators accept no loaded materials.

Collection buttons show only the process's input/output item types, plus actual buffered leftovers from earlier recipes so those materials remain recoverable. Unrelated recipes do not add visible buttons. Invisible, inaccessible reservations preserve the controls' wrapped height across recipe/stock changes. A transfer requests a positive safe-integer quantity, including quantities larger than one stack; the atomic inventory rules combine source stacks and fill destination stacks subject to actual capacity. Insufficient total stock or destination capacity rejects the whole transfer without spending anything.

**Collect all output** moves every currently stored output stack into the bag in one atomic worker command, independently of the material/quantity fields. It uses stock at command execution, combines matching bag stacks and rejects the entire collection if anything cannot fit. Input stock and pending job results stay with the machine; collection adds no production history. The button remains in place and disabled while output is empty. Existing reach and handcraft-exit protections apply.

## Mixed recycling

Each operation commits eight scrap and takes eight full-power seconds. Shuffle the following five equally weighted outcomes without replacement using the machine's saved xorshift32 stream; then reshuffle the next bag. Save the permutation, cursor, nonzero unsigned state and already-selected job output. A full output buffer, inspection, pause or save/load does not roll again.

| Plates | Wire | Returned scrap | Refined solid value | Total solid value |
| ------ | ---- | -------------- | ------------------- | ----------------- |
| 3      | 1    | 1              | 7                   | 8                 |
| 1      | 4    | 2              | 6                   | 8                 |
| 2      | 3    | 1              | 7                   | 8                 |
| 0      | 6    | 2              | 6                   | 8                 |
| 3      | 0    | 2              | 6                   | 8                 |

There is only one plate-free and one wire-free outcome in each five-result bag. Across a shuffle boundary, at most two consecutive batches can omit either material, so **every three consecutive completed batches contain both plates and wire**. Useful refined yield is at least six scrap-value units per batch; averages are 1.8 plates, 2.8 wire and 1.6 returned scrap. Every outcome satisfies `2 × plates + wire + returned scrap = 8`. Returned scrap is residual input and earns no production/extraction history. The complete outcome must fit the output buffer atomically. Contents never spill automatically.

## Power, extraction and shared research

Use watts, joules and simulation seconds. A day lasts 240 s and begins at phase 60 s (solar maximum). For saved tick `t`, let `s = 0.05 × t + 60`. Solar fraction is `max(0, sin(2πs / 240))`; wind fraction is `0.5 + 0.5 × sin(s / 19) × sin(s / 31)`, giving turbine output `250 + 250 × fraction` W. These curves have no wall-clock/random dependency. No batteries or Dunkelflaute events exist in this slice.

Explicit edges form separate connected components. Generate each source once, including cyclic networks. Allocate each runnable consumer `requested × min(1, supply / total runnable demand)`. Missing-input, disabled, idle and blocked consumers request no productive power. Work scales by the fraction and useful work caps actual energy consumption; unused generation/allocation is curtailed. A solar panel plus turbine produces up to 850–1,100 W at solar maximum; the recycler, both fabrication tiers, extractor and two labs request 620 W together. The wind floor alone permits a 250/620 = 40.3% shared rate at night when all six consumers are runnable. Shortage retains work and does not create a fail state.

An extractor completes up to four scrap in two full-power seconds, limited by the finite deposit's current remainder. Its prospective result still belongs to the deposit until the atomic deposit/output/history commit; dismantling an uncommitted extraction job cannot refund that result. Manual salvage can consume unreserved remaining material, and the extractor revalidates quantity at commit. Depletion and blocked output never duplicate material.

Each powered supplied lab contributes up to 4× field work from four local input slots. The existing single-head coordinator retains technology-owned partial work, stable lab ordering, source-local complete bundles and payment at cycle boundaries. It reports accepted work for energy accounting. Field Research remains power-free, uses uncommitted bag stock after manual/handcraft/MiniFab commitments and can finish every prototype technology. Queue edits never erase or redirect investment.

## Bootstrap and recovery accounting

All 14 research payments have 246 scrap of embodied solid value. A prybar and base MiniFab add 18. One of each placed kind, a second lab and eight power connections add 230, for **494 scrap of embodied construction/research value** before operating stock. This is a conservation/reachability budget, not a timing claim; recycling can return scrap for another processing pass. The generous 250,000-scrap start provides headroom for operation, experiments and extra components. Four lab-funded Basic kits are already included in the 246 research-payment total. No blueprint needs its own machine, kit or coal to bootstrap.

Dismantling preflights the complete return into the bag: body and attached-wire scrap value, intact input/output buffers, and unfinished committed ingredients or completed pending manufactured output exactly once. Reject insufficient capacity without changing work, topology or placement. Extraction's uncommitted prospective output remains in its deposit. Research investment is an irreversible sink. Existing crafted output history is retained; recovery, residual scrap and subsequent collection add no histories. Each machine has one operation-sized pending-result buffer in addition to its main output slots. Finishing manufactured work commits output/history to that pending buffer once, using a saved marker; if the main output buffer is full, the pending result waits without further power or rerolls. Moving it into main output, collecting it or recovering it never credits history again. An unfinished job's original inputs and a completed job's pending result are alternative ownership representations, not two sets of recoverable materials.
